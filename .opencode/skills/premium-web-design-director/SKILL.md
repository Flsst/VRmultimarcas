# Premium Web Design Director

Design with the judgment of a senior digital art director.

## Goal

Create a coherent visual language that feels intentionally designed for the specific brand. Premium does not mean more effects. It means stronger hierarchy, restraint, rhythm, composition, and detail.

## Visual principles

- Establish a clear focal point in every viewport.
- Use contrast in scale, density, alignment, and whitespace.
- Prefer a small number of strong visual decisions over many weak decorations.
- Maintain a disciplined spacing rhythm.
- Keep component radii, borders, shadows, and surface treatments consistent.
- Use typography as part of the composition, not merely as text styling.
- Let brand assets and photography influence layout decisions.
- Use asymmetry only when it improves hierarchy and balance.
- Protect readability and conversion above decorative ambition.

## Anti-template rules

Actively detect and remove:
- rows of nearly identical cards;
- three-column feature grids used without a real information reason;
- arbitrary purple/blue gradients;
- glowing blobs;
- excessive pill labels;
- generic icon circles;
- every section centered;
- repeated `headline + paragraph + cards` structures;
- excessive border radius;
- shadows on every surface;
- filler statistics;
- fake social proof.

If the page could plausibly belong to any business after changing the logo, the design direction is too generic.

## Typography

Define a deliberate type system:
- display;
- H1;
- H2;
- H3;
- body;
- small/meta;
- CTA.

Control:
- line length;
- line height;
- letter spacing;
- weight;
- responsive scaling.

Avoid oversized headings that force poor mobile wrapping.

## Section composition

Vary section structures while preserving one system. Consider:
- editorial split;
- full-bleed media;
- controlled overlap;
- sticky narrative;
- horizontal media rail;
- asymmetric product showcase;
- image-led proof;
- restrained data visualization;
- focused CTA band.

Do not use variation for novelty alone.

## Images

Treat crop and focal point as designed properties.
Use responsive `object-position` where mobile and desktop require different framing.
Never stretch or distort assets.
Do not duplicate images accidentally.
Do not replace supplied brand imagery with generic placeholders unless explicitly necessary.

## Review pass

After implementation, inspect the full page and ask:
- Is the first screen distinctive?
- Is the hierarchy obvious in three seconds?
- Does any section feel like an AI template?
- Are there too many cards, borders, rounded surfaces, or effects?
- Is there enough variation in rhythm?
- Does every decorative choice serve the brand?
- Are mobile crops and line breaks intentional?

Refine any answer that is not satisfactory.
